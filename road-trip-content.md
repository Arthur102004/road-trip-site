## Vegas on a Budget

Strategy: stack the free stuff daily, pick one cheap thing per day, and vote as a group on one or two splurges for the whole trip (splitting one splurge five ways beats five separate ones).

### Free: do all of these
- **Bellagio Conservatory & Botanical Gardens**: stunning seasonal displays inside the Bellagio.
- **Fountains of Bellagio**: shows every 15-30 min in the evening; the classic free Vegas moment.
- **Fremont Street Experience & Viva Vision**: downtown light-canopy show, plus cheap food around it.
- **AREA15 entry**: free to walk the immersive district with a pre-booked pass; pay only for what you enter.
- **Pinball Hall of Fame**: free entry, bring quarters, hundreds of machines.
- **Seven Magic Mountains**: neon boulder stacks 25 min south; quick photo run.
- **Ethel M Chocolate Factory & Cactus Garden**: free self-guided tour, free samples, big cactus garden.

### Cheap: under $40
- **Spider-Man: Brand New Day**: out Jul 31, brand new while you're in town. IMAX at Red Rock or Dolby at Town Square. ~$15-25.
- **Go-karting**: Vegas Superkarts or Pole Position Raceway. ~$26-35 per race.
- **Flavortown all-you-can-eat brunch**: Guy Fieri's bottomless Strip brunch. ~$35. The budget feast.
- **Neon Museum**: boneyard of old Vegas signs, best at night. ~$21, student discount at the door.
- **The Mob Museum**: organized-crime history downtown. ~$30-35, student rate available.
- **Las Vegas Natural History Museum**: cheapest museum in town. ~$12.
- **SlotZilla zipline**: fly under the Fremont lights. $25 lower line / $45 zoomline.

### Group splurges: vote on 1-2
- **Omega Mart (AREA15)**: surreal supermarket hiding a whole world. ~$45-54, book ahead.
- **Mountain biking at Lee Canyon**: lift-served downhill, 20-30° cooler up the mountain. ~$60-90 with rental.
- **The Sphere**: Wizard of Oz experience. ~$95-140.
- **Maverick Helicopters**: Strip night flight. ~$99-150 per person.

## Tesla Model Y Playbook (safety & charging)

**Charging & range.** Drive by the in-car Trip Planner: it routes you through Superchargers and warms the battery for fast charging before arrival. Back it up with A Better Routeplanner (ABRP) and PlugShare for live status. Charge to 100% the night before each big day and aim to reach each Supercharger with about 20% left. Loaded with five people, climbing mountains, and running AC, real range runs well under the dash estimate, so plan conservatively.

**Beating the desert heat.** Each morning while still plugged in, use Scheduled Departure to pre-cool the cabin on grid power, not the battery, so you leave full and cold. Keep Cabin Overheat Protection on when parked. Driving, keep windows up and climate on Auto. At stops, park and charge in shade; in extreme heat the car cools its battery before it will charge fast.

**Learn the car first.** It's a rental, so spend 20 minutes in a parking lot: door handles, drive/reverse, one-pedal regen braking, opening the charge port, plugging into a Supercharger. Set up phone keys for each driver and update the software before departure. Treat Autopilot as assistance only, hands on the wheel. Find the tire repair kit; there is no spare.

**On-road safety.** Swap drivers every 2-3 hours and never push through drowsiness. Seatbelts for all five, every leg. Carry more water than feels necessary (the desert is unforgiving if you break down), plus a first-aid kit, flashlight, and snacks. August is monsoon season in New Mexico and Arizona: watch for afternoon storms and never drive through a flooded wash. Download offline maps for dead-signal stretches.

**Build charging into the schedule.** Supercharger stops run 20-40 minutes versus five for gas, so fold them into meals and leg-stretches. It nudges arrival times later, especially on the long return days.
